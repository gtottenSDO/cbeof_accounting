- Publisher: Colorado Workforce Development Council (within CDLE).
- Released Dec 16, 2025. The 2026 edition is expected in Dec 2026.
- Report PDF: https://cochamber.com/wp-content/uploads/2025-Talent-Pipeline-Report.pdf

- Appendices: https://cwdc.colorado.gov/2025-talent-pipeline-report-appendices

## Relevant figures

Page numbers are from the PDF text; verify against the PDF.

**Accountants and auditors**
- Tier 1 Top Job (Tier 1 wage threshold: $86,320 a year or more).
- 3,793 projected annual openings, 2024–34.
- Ranks 3rd of the 60 Tier 1 Top Jobs by openings, behind software developers (4,583) and registered nurses (4,226). Ranks 4th across both tiers; sales representatives of services (4,827, Tier 2) is also ahead (Fig. 25–26).
- 18% of those openings come from industry growth (Fig. 25, p. 53).
- Caution: this 18% is the share of openings from growth, not an employment growth rate. The O*NET/Projections Central "+18%" is the 10-year employment change. Same number, different measures.

**Bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks**
- 3,215 annual openings. Not a Top Job.
- Typical entry requirement: some college, no degree (Fig. 30, p. 60).

**Payroll clerks and tax preparers.** Not mentioned in the report.

**Business and financial operations occupations**
- Employment share rose from 3.8% (2000) to 9.3% (2024).
- Location quotient 1.39 (pp. 18–20).
- 15 Top Jobs, 9.2% of all Top Jobs.

**Professional and technical services**
- 10.1% of employment in 2024, up from 7.0% in 2000. Location quotient 1.43.
- Lost 4,200 jobs from Aug 2023 to Aug 2025 (p. 38).
- Online job postings, Jan–Aug 2025: 51,199 (Fig. 20–21, pp. 42–44). Change: +1.7% vs 2024, −17.8% vs 2019, −41.8% vs 2022.

**Labor force**
- Workers 55+ were 20.8% of the 2024 labor force, up from under 11% in 2000 (p. 22).
- Exits, such as retirements, account for 36% of all projected openings (p. 52).

**Not found:** no AI analysis specific to accounting (the AI headwinds section, p. 107+, wasn't captured), and nothing on the CPA 150-hour rule or credential completions in accounting.

## Citation style in the 2026 PBS chapter

Two patterns:
1. **News and articles:** a parenthetical at the end of the sentence, with curly quotes:
   (“Title,” Author, Outlet, Month Day, Year).
   - The author is omitted when there is no byline, e.g. (“Microsoft to Cut About 4% of Jobs Amid Hefty AI Bets,” Reuters, July 2, 2025).
2. **Reports, surveys and government data:** named in the sentence. Examples:
   - "according to CBRE’s Scoring Tech Talent 2025 report"
   - "The Q3 2025 Quarterly Business and Economic Indicators report from the Colorado Secretary of State’s Office cited…"
   - "according to a WARN letter filed with the state"
   - "per Colorado Lawyer"

Also:
- No footnotes, URLs or reference list.
- Employment and wage figures (QCEW/CES) are not cited at all.
