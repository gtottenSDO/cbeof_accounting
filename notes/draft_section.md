Accounting and Payroll Services employment grew at a compound annual rate of 2.5% between 2015 and 2025, faster than Colorado overall (1.5%) but slower than the PST sector as a whole (3.6%). That growth has since reversed. Employment fell 0.9% in 2025 to 23,796, a steeper decline than for PST (-0.1%) or the state (-0.2%), and first-quarter 2026 employment was 6.2% below a year earlier. These losses have fallen almost entirely on younger workers. Between 2023 and 2025, employment of workers ages 22–24 in the industry in Colorado fell 19.7% and employment of workers ages 25–34 fell 9.0%, while employment of workers 55 and older was essentially unchanged, according to the U.S. Census Bureau’s Quarterly Workforce Indicators. Across all Colorado industries, employment in both younger age groups was roughly flat. The decline reflects reduced hiring rather than layoffs: separation rates for younger workers held steady, while expanding firms added 19% fewer positions for workers ages 25–34 between 2022 and 2024.

Offices of certified public accountants (CPAs), the industry’s largest component, account for most of the recent decline. First-quarter 2026 employment at CPA offices fell 9.3% year-over-year even as the number of firms rose 3.8%, a pattern consistent with consolidation and the automation of entry-level work. Roughly a quarter of the nation’s 100 largest CPA firms are now backed by private equity, including Denver-based Richey May (“Accounting Enters Its Private Equity Flip Era,” CFO Brew, April 30, 2026). National research finds that employment among workers ages 22–25 in the occupations most exposed to AI, including accountants, fell 13% relative to less-exposed workers (“Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence,” Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, and Ruyu Chen, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, August 2025). Long-term demand remains solid. According to the Colorado Workforce Development Council’s 2025 Colorado Talent Pipeline Report, accountants and auditors are a Tier 1 Top Job with an estimated 3,793 openings annually through 2034. Beginning January 1, 2027, SB26-076 will allow candidates to qualify for CPA certification with a bachelor’s degree and two years of experience as an alternative to the 150-credit-hour requirement.

Payroll services has followed a different path. Colorado employment in the subsector grew 4.1% annually from 2015 to 2025, more than double the national rate, and rose 2.5% in 2025 to 2,891 workers, though first-quarter 2026 employment was down 6.1% from a year earlier. Growing compliance complexity continues to support demand for outsourced payroll. Beginning with tax year 2026, employers must report qualified overtime and tipped-occupation codes on Form W-2 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (“IRS Finalizes 2026 Form W-2,” Experian Employer Services, January 14, 2026), in addition to state requirements such as Colorado’s FAMLI premium, set at 0.88% of wages for 2026. At the same time, the industry is consolidating around large software platforms, as shown by Paychex’s $4.1 billion acquisition of Paycor, and automation is reducing the need for routine processing. Colorado employment of payroll and timekeeping clerks is projected to decline 6% through 2034.
